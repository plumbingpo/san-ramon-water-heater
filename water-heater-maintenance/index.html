The inside of a neglected water heater tells the whole story. On the bottom there is a bed of mineral sediment, sometimes several inches deep, that makes the burner work harder, creates popping and rumbling, and can burn out the lower element on an electric unit. Hanging from the top is the anode rod, built to corrode in place of the tank, which may be down to bare wire after a few years. When it is used up, corrosion goes after the tank lining, and a leak is only a matter of time. For homeowners in San Ramon, CA, a yearly visit is the simplest way to keep both of those problems from shortening the life of the heater.

Our maintenance visit is built to interrupt both problems. We drain and flush the tank until the water runs clear, remove and check the anode rod and recommend a replacement when it is mostly consumed, and test the temperature and pressure relief valve to confirm it opens and reseats. On gas units we inspect the burner, pilot or igniter, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue baffle, and venting for proper draft. On electric units we check element resistance and thermostat operation and look at the wiring for heat damage. We check the expansion tank's air charge against your water pressure, confirm the thermostat setting is near 120 degrees to limit scalding, and look for early leaks at fittings and valves.

We maintain tankless units as well. In place of a sediment flush, they need periodic descaling, since hard water leaves mineral deposits in the heat exchanger that cut flow and efficiency. We isolate the unit, circulate a descaling solution through it, clean the inlet filter, check the vent and condensate line, and look at the error history for patterns. Whatever type you have, you receive a plain summary of the unit's condition and any parts approaching the end of their life. For water heater maintenance in San Ramon, call (925) 222-4901.

Signs You Need Water Heater Maintenance in San Ramon

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank
  • Temperature swings or reduced flow from a tankless heater
  • Shorter showers before the water turns lukewarm
  • Rusty or discolored hot water at the tap
  • Noises from the water heater that were not there before
  • A rotten-egg smell from the hot water
  • Energy bills creeping up with no change in hot water use
  • Small drips at the drain valve or connections

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

The homeowner's side of maintenance is short: keep the area clear, watch for drips, and pay attention to new sounds. The rest carries more risk than it looks. Flushing involves scalding water and an old plastic drain valve that may crack or refuse to seal. Anode rods are often seized in place and need significant force, sometimes with a flexible rod for low-clearance spaces. An older T&P valve may not reseat once tested, which leaves you with a drip and a replacement to arrange. And on gas heaters, the burner and vent affect how combustion gases leave the house, which is not something to guess about.

Professional maintenance also brings the right parts on the truck, including drain valves, anode rods and flexible anodes, relief valves, and meters for electrical testing. Tankless descaling gear comes along for those units. Beyond the tools, experience matters: an experienced eye can spot a corroding nipple, a sooty burner, or an expansion tank that has lost its charge well before any of them cause trouble. San Ramon Water Heater treats maintenance as a way to catch small issues early and keep your heater working for as many years as possible.

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Glance at the pan and connections regularly so small leaks are caught early
  • Schedule a flush every year, or every six months if you have hard water
  • Stay on top of tankless descaling to avoid error codes and reduced flow
  • Keep the thermostat near 120 degrees to limit scalding and slow sediment buildup
  • Treat hard water to cut down on sediment and scale
  • Keep at least a couple of feet clear around the heater for air and service access
  • Ask about the anode rod at each visit; it is the tank's main defense against rust
