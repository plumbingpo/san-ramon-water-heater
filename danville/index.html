Keeping Your Danville Water Heater Running Longer

Most early failures start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and operate the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually encodes the manufacture date, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that range and also shows discolored water, rumbling, or moisture at the base, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet that same buildup, ignored for a few years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and burn out the lower element on an electric model, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number on your phone today so you can reach us fast the moment something seems off. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.
