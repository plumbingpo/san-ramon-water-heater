A tankless water heater keeps no reserve of hot water. It waits for a faucet to open, senses the flow, fires a burner or energizes its elements, and heats water as it races through a compact heat exchanger. That on-demand design is efficient and space-saving, but it packs a lot of sensors and controls into one small cabinet, and every one of them has to agree before you get hot water. When one of them objects, the unit shuts down and posts an error code. In San Ramon, CA, the usual culprits are mineral scale coating the heat exchanger, a flow sensor that no longer registers demand, an igniter or flame rod that cannot prove the flame, and venting or gas supply problems that trip the unit's safety limits.

San Ramon Water Heater starts every tankless repair by reading the unit, not guessing at it. On every service call in San Ramon, we pull the active error code and the stored fault history, then test the components that code points to: flow sensor output while a faucet runs, flame rod signal in microamps, igniter resistance, inlet gas pressure both static and at full fire, and vent condition from the unit to the termination. Scale gets checked by comparing inlet and outlet temperatures against flow rate, since a heat exchanger that cannot transfer heat shows up clearly in those numbers. A code tells us where to look, not what to replace. An ignition code, for example, can come from a worn igniter, a dirty flame rod, low gas pressure, or a blocked intake, and each one calls for a different fix.

The good news is that most tankless problems are very fixable. A descaling flush clears most mineral buildup, sensors and igniters are replaceable parts, and venting faults usually come down to a blocked intake or a loose joint. We carry common parts for the major brands and service both gas and electric units, so the typical repair is done in one trip. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Repeated ignition attempts, then a shutdown
  • No hot water at a low-flow faucet even though the shower works fine
  • A combustion fan that sounds louder or rougher than normal
  • Water dripping from the cabinet or the condensate line
  • The display showing an error code instead of a temperature
  • A cold-water sandwich that has gotten noticeably worse
  • Lukewarm water whenever two fixtures run at the same time
  • Water that swings between hot and lukewarm in the middle of a shower

The Benefits of Professional Tankless Water Heater Repair

Tankless repair is a poor fit for DIY for two reasons: the diagnosis is tricky and the hazards are real. On the diagnostic side, an ignition code might mean a worn igniter, a dirty flame rod, low gas pressure, or a blocked intake, and replacing parts until the code clears adds up fast. On the safety side, a gas unit burns fuel at a very high rate and vents exhaust that contains carbon monoxide, while an electric unit can pull more than 100 amps through multiple 240-volt breakers. Errors with either type can be serious.

Professional service is usually the cheaper path, too. Our technicians do not guess at error codes. They test flow sensors with water moving, measure flame rod signal, confirm igniter resistance, read inlet gas pressure at full fire, and check the vent from end to end. When scale is the problem, we flush it out properly and rinse the system before the unit goes back in service. And because some tankless problems, like an undersized gas line or a cracked heat exchanger, are not solved by parts at all, we tell you so directly, before you commit to anything.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here are our recommendations:

  • Rinse the small filter screen on the cold inlet every few months
  • Schedule a descaling flush every year; hard water may call for one every six months
  • Protect the heat exchanger with a softener or scale inhibitor when your water is hard
  • Write down any error code before resetting the unit, and call if it keeps coming back
  • Tell us before adding a gas appliance, so the gas line can be checked for the extra load
  • Clear debris, snow, and dryer lint away from the vent and intake openings
  • Leave the power on in cold weather, since freeze protection only runs when the unit has electricity
