Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Pleasanton

Most early failures trace back to maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Flush the tank every year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and test the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year to protect the heat exchanger.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like discolored hot water, popping noises, or a damp spot around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new heater after a leak.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Consider a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water begins corroding the tank steel, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a surprise cleanup.
