Most gas water heaters in San Ramon, CA work the same basic way. Gas enters through a control valve that doubles as the thermostat and feeds a burner beneath the tank, and the hot exhaust travels up a flue through the middle of the tank and out through the vent. A thermocouple, or a flame sensor on newer electronic models, tells the valve a flame is present; without that signal, the valve shuts off the gas for safety. The design is dependable, but it has a few predictable weak spots. Thermocouples wear out, pilot tubes and burner ports clog, the flame arrestor screen at the base packs up with lint and dust, control valves fail, and vents lose their draft.

Every gas water heater call starts with safety and then moves to testing. If there is a gas odor, leave the home first, call the gas utility from outside, and do not operate switches or try to relight anything. Once the area is safe, our San Ramon technicians work through the heater systematically: millivolt readings on the thermocouple, a close look at the pilot and main burner flames, gas pressure at the control valve, a draft test to make sure exhaust is not spilling back, and a CO check around the appliance. Scorch marks at the burner access, rust flakes on the burner, and a lazy yellow flame each point to a different problem, and we chase down the right one.

Most gas water heater repairs are finished in a single visit. We replace thermocouples, flame sensors, pilot assemblies, and igniters, clean burners and flame arrestor screens, swap failed gas control valves, and correct vent connectors that have separated or lost their slope. We also flush sediment, the cause of that popping or rumbling sound and a major reason tanks overheat at the bottom and fail early. When the tank itself is leaking or the heater is deep into its service life, we tell you honestly and walk you through replacement. New installations are sized by first-hour rating, not just gallons, and include the right venting, a sediment trap on the gas line, an expansion tank where needed, and seismic straps and a drain pan where code requires them. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Showers that go cold sooner than they did a year ago
  • Loud rumbling from the bottom of the tank
  • Pilot outages that keep coming back after a relight
  • A vent pipe that has separated, rusted, or left scorch marks near the top of the heater
  • Rust-colored hot water or rust streaks on the tank
  • No hot water at all, with the burner never firing
  • A flame that looks orange or floppy rather than steady and blue
  • Any smell of gas around the appliance, which calls for leaving and phoning the gas utility

The Benefits of Professional Gas Water Heater Services

Two things make gas water heater work a poor DIY project: the parts involve fuel and combustion, and the symptoms overlap. A pilot outage might come from a worn thermocouple, a partly blocked pilot tube, lint on the flame arrestor screen, a downdraft, or an internal valve failure. Guess wrong and you have spent money without fixing anything. Guess wrong on a gas fitting or vent connection and the stakes are much higher, since a small leak or a spillage problem can put gas or carbon monoxide into the living space. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Vacuum lint and dust from the flame arrestor screen at the base a few times a year
  • Replace a spent anode rod before the tank itself starts to rust
  • Keep working CO alarms near bedrooms and on each floor
  • Keep the thermostat around 120 degrees to save energy and reduce scalding risk
  • Watch for a loose, rusted, or sagging vent pipe above the heater
  • Give the heater breathing room and keep flammables well away from it
  • Flush a few gallons from the drain valve periodically to control sediment
